# Transform polish: silent conversion, click-and-double-click sliders, a visible reference box for the typed move

Status: Ready
Priority: Should
Origin: Customer (three change requests of 2026-10-10). Criteria marked (Proposal) are the product owner's additions; they are built with the slice and the customer can veto them at the demo, as with `0044`. None of them is needed for the three requests.
Depends on: `0019-multi-object-transform` merged (PR #80: the group box, the multi-object skew and stretch rules, the group move chip) and `0017-style-panel-rework` merged (the colour picker and the Select-bar slider). Runs before `0044`, `0043` and `0020` or after them, never beside them: it touches `session/keys.rs`, the commit labels and the move chip, which they touch too (`adrs.md`).

## User value

As a maker I want to skew or stretch any selection without first converting shapes to paths and without being told off for it, so that the tool does what I asked and I can see afterwards that a path is what a skewed shape is.

As a maker I want to click a slider where I want the value and double-click it when I want to type the value, so that every slider in the app behaves the same way and I never have to aim at a thumb.

As a maker I want the typed move to measure from the centre of the selection by default, to see the true box the numbers refer to, and to pick any corner or the centre as the reference by clicking it, so that "move this corner to 100, 50" and "put the middle at 100, 50" are one gesture each.

The customer's words (translated, 2026-10-10):

1. "Allow the conversion of primitives to paths on skew and on multi-object transform without any warning. Don't forbid or warn unnecessarily."
2. "For every slider: a single click on the track sets the value to the pointer position, a double-click enters value entry, as elsewhere in the app. Dragging stays as it is."
3. "When I move by a typed value, the default reference point is the top-left corner. It must become the centre of the selection's bounding box. The true bounding box is invisible today: show it in pale red while dragging and while the double-click entry is open. In the absolute-value entry show the four corners and the centre cross of that box in red; they can be clicked to choose the reference point. Plain Return uses the centre. Clicking a corner or the centre cross executes the move with that point as the reference."

**Field reference.** Inkscape converts nothing on skew: a skewed rectangle stays a `rect` with a `transform` matrix in the SVG, and "Object to Path" (Shift+Ctrl+C) bakes it later; its X/Y fields in the selector bar measure from the top-left of the box and offer no choice of point. Illustrator's Transform panel has a 9-point reference-point locator next to X/Y. LightBurn's Numeric Edits toolbar has a 9-dot control that sets which point of the selection's rectangle X/Y refer to, and also the anchor of a scale and the centre of a rotation ([LightBurn docs](https://docs.lightburnsoftware.com/2.1/Reference/NumericEditsToolbar/)). In both the chosen point is a persistent setting that is easy to forget, and the box it refers to is not drawn. Where we do better: the box the numbers refer to is drawn while the maker types; the point is chosen by clicking the very spot on the drawing, and the click also executes, so there is no hidden setting to forget; the default is the middle, which is what a maker means by "put this at 100, 50"; and a skewed or stretched shape becomes a path in the same step, where Inkscape needs a second command to bake the matrix. For the sliders, the tools in this field mostly rely on the platform's native range control, so a click jumps and nothing opens on a double-click; the double-click entry is ours.

## What exists today

Facts from the specs and the code on `main` (2026-10-10); #80 is assumed merged, the implementer verifies against it.

- **Skew and stretch refuse primitives; nothing is converted anywhere except by the "Object to path" button.** A rectangle, ellipse, polygon or star shows no skew handle; nothing happens if a skew is tried (`0008` criteria 50 to 53, decision P1 of 2026-10-06, P2 "auto-convert on first use" rejected then; `0009` criterion 17 and question 5). A multi-selection holding a polygon, a star or a rotated rectangle or ellipse can only be scaled proportionally; the edge handles are not drawn; the corner handle's hint chip says "Resize selection, proportional only", names the cause ("Holds a star ...") and says "To stretch: Object to path first"; the tooltip of "Object to path" says "Paths can be stretched and skewed freely" (`0019` criteria 21, 38, 39, question 2). A multi-selection with any primitive shows no skew handle and K shows "Skew works on paths only" (`0019` criteria 37, 40, decision D4). `0003` criterion 21 and `0005` criterion 21 forbid any implicit conversion. So the customer's "today it converts" is not what the specs say: today it **refuses**; this spec turns the refusals into a silent conversion.
- **Four sliders exist**, listed in the table of Part B. The two colour-picker controls already jump to the pointer on press and drag from there; the two native range inputs do the browser's own thing (a click on the track jumps in WebKit, Blink and Gecko). No slider opens a value entry on a double-click. The value fields (Stroke width, the two opacities, the marker count) are `role="spinbutton"` controls, not sliders: a click types, a drag scrubs (`0017` criteria 34 to 45).
- **The typed move** (`0010` criteria 15 to 25) has a Relative and an Absolute mode. Absolute puts the **top-left** corner of the tight outline bounds at (X, Y); untouched fields show that corner's current X and Y; the chip opens in Relative. The tight bounds are not drawn. The selection box that is drawn is the dashed blue oriented box (of a rotated object, a different rectangle), or for a multi-selection the group box, which has the same bounds as the tight bounds (`0019` criterion 1). There is no X/Y readout of an object anywhere in the Properties panel (the Style and Document tabs have none; `0008` lists it as out of scope) and the status bar shows the **pointer** position only.

## Terms

- **Primitive**: rectangle, ellipse, polygon, star. **Aligned primitive**: as in `0019` Terms (a rectangle or ellipse whose rotation is a multiple of 90°, or a circle at any rotation; never a polygon or star).
- **Conversion**: exactly what "Object to path" does for one primitive (`0003` criteria 17 to 20, `0013` criterion 16, `0012` criterion 16): same function, same nodes, node kinds and node count (a rectangle 4 or, rounded, 8; an ellipse 4; a polygon N; a star 2N), same style, same place in the stacking order. This spec adds no second conversion.
- **Stretch**: a scale with factors sx and sy that differ by more than 1e-9. A scale with |sx - sy| <= 1e-9 is **uniform**.
- **True bounding box (TBB)**: the tight axis-parallel bounds in document space of the drawn outline of the selected object, or of all selected objects together, without stroke width: the box `0010` criterion 21 uses for the typed absolute move and `0019` criterion 1 calls the group box. It is not the oriented box and not an object's unrotated frame. Millimetres, X right, Y down, origin at the document's top-left corner (as the rulers).
- **Reference points**: the four corners of the TBB (top left, top right, bottom left, bottom right) and its centre `((min.x+max.x)/2, (min.y+max.y)/2)`. Nine points (edge midpoints) are not offered (question 8).
- **Mark**: the on-canvas drawing of a reference point. **Slider**: a control that sets a value by a position on a track; the four of the table in Part B.
- Lengths are compared with the document's geometric tolerance, angles with 1e-9 rad, as in the earlier specs.

## Acceptance criteria

### Part A: skew and stretch convert primitives, silently

**What converts.** Skew: every selected rectangle, ellipse, polygon and star. Stretch of a selection of two or more objects: a polygon, a star, and a rectangle or ellipse that is not an aligned primitive. Nothing else: paths, aligned rectangles and ellipses, circles, and every object of a uniform scale, a rotate, a move or a copy keep their kind.

1. Given the Select tool and a selection of one object or several, of any mix of kinds (path, rectangle, ellipse, polygon, star), when the selection's box is drawn, then four skew handles are shown with the layout, glyph, 24 px lever rule, size tiers and degenerate-axis rules of `0008` criteria 37 and 48 and `0019` criteria 12 and 40. No kind hides them.
2. Given a skew drag (`0008` criteria 38 to 40, `0019` criterion 41) on a selection that holds primitives, when the maker releases, then each primitive has become a path that is the exact image of its outline under the shear, within the geometric tolerance, and every path of the selection is sheared as today. Example: one 10 x 10 mm rectangle with its top-left corner at (0, 0), top skew handle, bottom side fixed, angle 45°: the result is one closed path with 4 corner nodes at (10, 0), (20, 0), (10, 10) and (0, 10), the style of the rectangle, and the selection box is the box of the skewed path.
3. Given the conversion in criterion 2 or 8, then the path is the one "Object to path" gives (same function): its node count, node kinds and handles before the map (criterion 2's example: 4 for a plain rectangle, 8 for a rounded one, 4 smooth nodes for an ellipse, N for a polygon, 2N for a star), its stroke and fill style (`0007` criterion 33), no markers (`0018` criterion 23), the identity rule and stacking-order slot that "Object to path" has, and it stays selected; no object leaves the selection and the box is refit from the result.
4. Given a release that converts one or more objects, then exactly one commit is made, the commit of the gesture (the skew or scale label of `0020`), for all selected objects together. It is atomic: no reader of the document, no saved file and no peer sees a converted but not yet transformed object, or some objects done and others not. There is no separate "Object to path" commit and no second commit for the conversion. Test: after the release the document version has advanced by one commit, and a replica that imports it sees either the old document or the final one.
5. Given a skew or scale drag that would convert, then the preview is the one of `unified-object-editing` (committed geometry in its own style, the result as the 1.5 px blue outline, no fill preview) and shows the converted, transformed outline of each primitive; the preview and the commit come from the same function. Nothing is converted or written until the release. A press and release under the 3 px dead zone, Escape, a drag back to the start, and a factor or angle of no change write nothing and convert nothing.
6. Given a selection of any kind and any mix, when the maker double-clicks a skew handle, or presses K (top handle) or Shift+K (right handle) with the Select tool active, then the skew chip of `0010` criteria 9 to 13 opens, and Enter skews by the typed angle exactly as a drag of that angle would, converting the primitives as criterion 2 says. The hint "Skew works on paths only" no longer exists; K with nothing selected still shows "Select an object first".
7. Given a selection of two or more objects, then all eight resize handles are drawn by the tiers of `0019` criterion 10 whatever the kinds are, and a corner drag without Ctrl stretches freely. Given a stretch (sx and sy differ by more than 1e-9) on such a selection, when the maker releases, then each polygon, each star, and each rectangle or ellipse that is not an aligned primitive is converted and then mapped as a path (anchors mapped, handle vectors scaled by (sx, sy) in the document axes, `0019` criterion 20 row "path"); aligned rectangles, aligned ellipses, circles and paths take the result of `0019` criterion 20 unchanged and keep their kind. Example: a closed 10 x 10 path at (0, 0) and a polygon with 4 points whose vertices are (25, 0), (30, 5), (25, 10) and (20, 5) (outer radius 5 mm, centre (25, 5); the order of the nodes is the one "Object to path" gives); the group box is (0, 0) to (30, 10); dragging the bottom-right corner from (30, 10) to (60, 30) gives sx = 2, sy = 3 about (0, 0): the path becomes (0, 0) to (20, 30) and the polygon becomes a path with 4 corner nodes at (50, 0), (60, 15), (50, 30) and (40, 15) in any order. The vertex positions are those "Object to path" gives for that polygon; the tester checks them against that command first. Given the same selection with an aligned rectangle added, then the rectangle is still a rectangle with sx and sy applied to its width and height.
8. Given a stretch commit that converts, then the rules of criterion 4 hold (one commit, atomic), and `0019` criteria 22 and 24 hold unchanged: factors clamp at 0, a result beyond +-1e7 mm is no change at all (nothing is converted, nothing is moved), "Scale stroke width" and "Scale corner radius" are read at the press, and a converted object's stroke width follows the switch like a path's does.
9. Given a multi-selection and the typed size (double-click a resize handle, or S), then the W and H fields are independent for every selection (the link of `0019` criterion 34 for a "uniform-only" selection is gone), and Enter converts and stretches by the rule of criterion 7. A size equal to the current one writes nothing.
10. Given a uniform scale (Ctrl on a corner handle, or typed W and H in the box's aspect ratio, so |sx - sy| <= 1e-9), a rotate, a move, a copy or a typed move, then no object is converted: a polygon stays a polygon with its outer radius times the factor (`0019` criterion 20). Rotating by Δ and then stretching converts only at the stretch.
11. Given any conversion by this spec, then no text tells the maker about it: no hint chip, toast, notice, dialog, status-bar text, badge, tooltip line or accessible announcement mentions a conversion, a path or "Object to path" in connection with a skew or stretch gesture. The strings "Skew works on paths only", "proportional only", "Holds a" and "To stretch: Object to path first" are gone from the UI. The hint chips of the skew and resize handles keep the lines of `0019` criterion 38 and `0008`, without the uniform-only row. The maker learns that a shape became a path from what it now offers: node editing on double-click, and the Select bar, which loses the kind group and shows "Object to path" only while a primitive is left in the selection.
12. Given exactly one polygon or star selected, then it keeps corner resize handles only and a corner drag stays uniform (`0005` criterion 11). Given exactly one rectangle or ellipse, then it keeps the oriented-box resize of `0005` and `0008` and is never converted by a resize. (Question 2.)
13. Given "Object to path" and its tooltip "Convert the selected shapes to paths", then they behave as before this spec; the button is not shown when the selection holds no primitive.
14. Given a project in which a skew or stretch converted objects, when the maker saves, closes and reopens it, then those objects are paths with the anchors, handles, `rotation`, style and stacking position they had, and the objects that were not converted are exactly as before. No new field is written and `format_version` does not change (a path is an existing kind).
15. Given `0020` is built, then the skew or stretch is one step with the operation name of that gesture ("Skew" or "Resize"), no "Object to path" row is added to the table, and undoing it brings back the primitives with every parameter (width, height, corner radii, rx, ry, point count, ratio, rotation) and the previous positions. Until `0020` exists the commit is final, as every commit is today.
16. Given a group (`0023`, when built), then a group transform that skews or stretches converts the primitives inside by the same rule; the group has no transform of its own (`0023` criteria 22, 23). No further criterion is needed here.

### Part B: sliders

The four sliders of today (verified by `type="range"` and `role="slider"` in `frontend/src`, 2026-10-10):

17. Given a **click**, meaning a press and a release of the primary button (or a touch or pen contact) on the same slider with the pointer moving less than 3 px (a touch: 8 px) between them, on the track and not on the thumb, then the slider's value is the value at the pointer position, rounded to the slider's step (S1, S2: 0.01; S3, S4: the 8-bit colour that position gives, as a drag gives it today), the thumb is there, and the change is written in one commit on release exactly as the end of a drag writes it (S1: all selected stars; S3 and S4: the RGB of all selected objects, each keeping its alpha; S2: the tool setting for new stars, which is not a document write). A slider may preview the value at the press (the native input does, and cannot do otherwise); what is tested is the end state and the single commit, not the absence of a preview between press and release.
18. Given a click on the **thumb** (within its drawn extent), then the value does not change and nothing is written; a drag from the thumb moves it from where it was grabbed, not from the pointer position (no jump of up to half the thumb at the press; the picker's thumbs behave as the native one). Test: click the hue thumb at its edge; the hue is what it was.
19. Given a **drag**, meaning a press followed by 3 px or more of movement (touch: 8 px), then everything is as before this spec: a drag that starts on the track sets the value under the pointer at the first move and follows it; the object previews per animation frame and one commit follows on release even if the pointer is outside the control; Escape during the drag drops the preview and the release writes nothing (S1, S3, S4: `0017` criterion 18, `0009` criterion 21); Home, End, arrow keys and the Shift step are unchanged (criterion 25).
20. Given a slider whose selected objects differ in value (S1 on stars with different ratios, S3 and S4 on objects with different colours), then it shows no thumb and its readout shows "Mixed" (`0009`, `0017` criterion 21), and a click on its track sets **all** selected objects to the clicked value in one commit.
21. Given a **double-click** (two clicks within the platform's double-click interval, as the engine reports it by a click count of 2, each moving less than 3 px, the second within 4 px of the first) on S1 or S2, on the track or on the thumb, then the readout beside the slider becomes a text field in place, with the slider's value selected, and the slider stays where it is. Enter commits the typed value in one commit (S1: all selected stars; S2: the tool setting) and closes the field; Escape, a press elsewhere or a blur closes it and writes nothing. The field is the shared typed-text field of the panel's value fields (`EntryField`: decimal point or comma accepted, error message in the same chip, `aria-invalid`). A press on the slider while the entry is open closes the entry without writing and is a click like any other (a third click of a triple click does not reopen it).
22. Given the entry of criterion 21, then the typed value must be a finite number from 0.01 to 0.99 (checked as typed, before the rounding) and is rounded to two decimals ("0.456" gives 0.46, "0.995" is refused); anything else keeps the field open, re-selects the text, marks it invalid and shows "Enter a number from 0.01 to 0.99", and writes nothing. On a mixed S1 the field is empty with the placeholder "Mixed", and a valid typed value is applied to all selected stars. A value equal to the current one, and an unedited Enter, write nothing.
23. Given a double-click on S3 or S4, then the hex field of the same colour block (Stroke, Fill or Background) takes keyboard focus with its whole text selected once the second click is released (not at the press, or the slider's own press focus would take it back), and nothing else opens. (Question 5.) The value entry for a colour is the hex field; there is no second field for a hue or a saturation.
24. Given the **first click of a double-click**, then it is a click like any other (criterion 17): it sets and commits the value at its position, and the entry of criterion 21 or 23 opens on the second click showing that value. The second press does not move the thumb: after a double-click the slider holds the value of the first click, even though the second press may lie up to 4 px away (on a 100 px track that is up to 4 steps). The double-click as a whole does not undo the first click. (Question 4.)
25. Given the **keyboard**, then arrow keys (1 step; S3 and S4: 1 % of the range, Shift 10 %), Home and End, and on the native sliders Page Up and Page Down, work exactly as before this spec, preview on key-down and commit on key-up so that a held key is one commit (`0007`, `0017` criterion 19). After Enter or Escape in an entry that was opened from the keyboard, focus returns to the slider; after a pointer-opened entry it returns to the canvas, as the value fields do (`onReturnFocus`). Keys typed in an entry never reach the canvas shortcuts or `Ctrl+Z` (`isFormControl` already lists inputs and `role="slider"`).
26. Given assistive technology, then each slider keeps its role, name, `aria-valuemin`, `aria-valuemax`, `aria-valuenow` and `aria-valuetext` ("Mixed" while mixed; S3 "Saturation 25 %, value 50 %"; S4 "Hue 180 degrees"), which are updated after a click, a drag, a key and a typed entry; S2 gains the name of the table; the entry field's accessible name is the slider's name; an error message is announced politely (the existing live region). The click and the double-click are pointer gestures; the keyboard equivalents are criterion 25.
27. Given a touch screen or a pen, then a tap on the track is a click and a double-tap (the same interval, 24 px apart at most) is a double-click; a tap must not zoom or scroll the page: the native sliders get `touch-action: manipulation`, the picker keeps `touch-action: none`. A drag on S3 or S4 does not scroll the panel.
28. (Proposal) Given a slider whose drawn track is under 24 px high (S4: 12 px, the native sliders), then its pointer target is 24 px high around the track (WCAG 2.5.8) without a change of the drawing: a click 6 px above or below the S4 track still sets the hue. The extension never overlaps the target of a neighbouring control (S4 has 8 px to the area above it and 8 px to the row below, so 6 px fits).
29. Given a slider with an entry open, then Escape closes only the entry (the Escape cascade of `0010` is not advanced: the selection stays), and the slider shows the value it had.
30. Given a slider that is not shown (nothing selected, Paint None), then it has no click or double-click; the rules for when a slider is shown are those of the earlier specs.
31. ### Part C: the typed move measures from the centre, and the box is visible

**Decisions, stated once** (the customer asked for each):

- **Relative and Absolute.** The reference point matters in Absolute only. Relative translates by (X, Y) as before; no mark is drawn and no Reference control is shown in Relative. The box is drawn in both modes.
- **Which box.** The TBB (Terms): the tight bounds of the drawn outline, the box the numbers already mean. For one object that is the object's own bounds; for a multi-selection, and for a group of `0023`, it is the group box of `0019` criterion 1 (they are the same rectangle). For a rotated object it is the bounds of the rotated outline, not the oriented box: both boxes are drawn, the blue dashed oriented one as today and the pale red true one, which is what makes the difference visible. The corners of the TBB of a rotated object can lie outside the shape; that is the point of "true".
- **The panel.** There is no X/Y readout of the selection in the Properties panel and none is added (the status bar's X/Y is the pointer). The chip's fields are the one place an object position is shown and they follow the chip's reference. If a position readout is ever added it uses the same default (Centre) and the same five points (question 9).
- **Zoom.** The box line and the marks are drawn in screen pixels at every zoom; the hit target is a screen-space circle of radius `min(12 px, 0.35 s)` (24 px across at normal sizes), `s` the shorter side of the box on screen; marks are drawn only from `s` of 24 px; the Reference control of criterion 41 always works.
- **Colour.** Pale red box line `--reference-box`, saturated red marks `--reference-mark`; contrast figures in the UX notes (marks 3.2:1 on the canvas and 3.9:1 on their white casing, the pale line about 2.0:1 and informational, question 10).

*The box*

33. Given a move drag of the selection (a plain move, a Shift axis lock, or a Ctrl copy) past the 3 px dead zone, then the TBB of the geometry the release would commit is drawn as a pale red box, in the frame of the first preview, and it moves with the preview in every frame; in a Ctrl copy it travels with the copy, while the blue selection box and handles stay on the originals (`0010` UX, "Modifier indicators"). It disappears at the release, at Escape, and when the drag ends where it began. Example: a 30 x 40 mm rectangle with its top-left corner at (10, 20) dragged by (+20, +10): the red box runs from (30, 30) to (60, 70) during the drag. (Scale, rotate and skew drags show no red box; question 7.)
34. Given the move chip of `0010` open (opened by double-click on the centre handle or by M), in Relative or Absolute mode, then the TBB of the selection at the time the chip opened is drawn as the pale red box for as long as the chip is open. It does not preview the typed values (typed values do not preview, `unified-object-editing`), and it goes with the chip on Enter, Escape, a press elsewhere, a tool switch, a selection change or a blur.
35. Given a selection, then the box is exact: it equals the TBB to the geometric tolerance. Example (`0019` criterion 1): a rectangle 20 x 10 mm with centre (10, 5) rotated 30° has the TBB x from -1.16 to 21.16 and y from -4.33 to 14.33 (to 0.01 mm), centre (10, 5); with a circle (rx = ry = 5) at centre (40, 5) added, the TBB is x from -1.16 to 45.00, y from -4.33 to 14.33, centre (21.92, 5.00). A stroke width does not change the box (`0019` criterion 2).
36. Given the box is drawn, then it is a 1 px solid line, pixel-aligned (never drawn at 50 %), in `--reference-box`, over a white casing, below the blue preview outline, the selection box and the handles, and above the artwork; it has no handles and no hit area; it does not hide, move or restyle the dashed blue selection box. Where it coincides with the blue box (an unrotated object, or a multi-selection) the red line shows through the gaps of the blue dashes. It is clipped to the viewport, and a move drag at the 10,000-object scale of `0019` criterion 49 meets that criterion's frame budget with the box drawn (it is computed from the preview bounds the group box uses, with no second pass over the objects).

*The reference and the marks*

37. Given the chip is open in **Absolute** mode, then X and Y are the position of the **selected reference point** of the TBB in document coordinates, and the selected reference is **Centre** every time the chip opens, whatever was chosen before. Untouched fields show that point's current X and Y with one decimal (the format of `0010` criterion 19). Example: a rectangle 30 x 40 mm with its top-left corner at (10, 20) has the TBB (10, 20) to (40, 60); the chip opened in Absolute shows X "25.0" and Y "40.0".
38. Given Absolute mode and Enter in any field or control, then the selection is translated so that the selected reference point lies at the typed position, by the same operation, in one commit, with the same writes as a drag of that offset (`0010` criterion 20), unchanged for the other cases of `0010` criteria 19, 22 and 25. A field the maker did not edit means "no change on that axis". Plain Enter, with the reference never changed, therefore uses the **centre**. Example: the rectangle above, typed 100 and 50: the centre is at (100, 50) and the top-left corner at (85, 30).
39. Given Absolute mode, then the chip's marks are drawn on the canvas: a small square at each of the four corners of the TBB and a cross at its centre, in `--reference-mark` with a white casing, at screen-constant size, above the artwork, the box and the handles, with the selected reference drawn as the current one (a filled mark; the others hollow). They are drawn only when `s` is at least 24 px and only for the part of the box inside the viewport (a mark outside it is not pulled in). In Relative mode no mark is drawn. Box and marks follow a pan, a zoom and a window resize while the chip is open (they are recomputed from the document, not drawn at the screen position of the chip's opening). A thin box (a straight line, a long narrow strip: shorter side under 24 px on screen) shows no marks at all; the Reference control of criterion 41 is its way.
40. Given the marks and a press and release within `min(12 px, 0.35 s)` of a mark (touch: 22 px, the same cap rule), nearest centre first, the centre cross winning a tie, then the move **executes** with that mark's point as the reference, in Absolute mode, using the typed text of the fields and the state of the Copy check, in one commit with the writes of criterion 38; the chip closes and focus returns to the canvas. A press on a mark is consumed by the chip: it does not cancel the chip, select, move or start a marquee. The action happens at the release; a press on a mark that moves 3 px or more before the release (touch: 8 px) is not a click: nothing happens and the chip stays open, and Escape between press and release drops the press. A press anywhere else cancels the chip and is processed as `0010` criterion 25 says, unchanged. Examples, each from the 30 x 40 rectangle above, typed 100 and 50: the top-left mark gives the top-left corner at (100, 50) (origin (100, 50)); the bottom-right mark puts the bottom-right corner at (100, 50) (origin (70, 10)); the centre cross is the same as Enter. Typed X = 100 only, then the top-right mark: the top-right corner's x becomes 100 and its y stays: origin (70, 20). Rotated example (criterion 35): typed 0 and 0 and the top-left mark moves the rectangle's centre to (11.16, 9.33). Multi-selection example (criterion 35): typed 0 and 0 and Enter moves every object by (-21.92, -5.00): the rectangle's centre goes to (-11.92, 0.00) and the circle's centre to (18.08, 0.00).
41. Given the chip is open in Absolute mode, then the chip also holds a **Reference** control, so the choice has a keyboard route and a name for a screen reader: a group with `role="radiogroup"`, accessible name "Reference point", five radios "Centre", "Top left", "Top right", "Bottom right", "Bottom left" (in that order), one Tab stop (roving: arrow keys move the selection, Left and Up to the previous option, Right and Down to the next, wrapping). Enter anywhere in the chip applies with the selected option. Selecting an option does not execute the move. (The control is a (Proposal) in form; a keyboard route is required by the design system's keyboard concept, `0019` UX section 13. Its look is the ux-engineer's.)

Enter uses the chosen point, the centre until you choose another. Click a marked point on the canvas to use it." (replacing "Top-left corner of the object's bounds ..." and, for a multi-selection, "Top-left corner of the selection"), and the accessible names of the fields stay "X position" and "Y position". No accessible name or description in this part names a colour.
43. Given the pointer rests on a mark, then the mark gets its hover state (larger by 2 px) and the cursor is `pointer`; after 600 ms a hint chip names it: "Top left corner" (likewise the other three, and "Centre") and "Click: move it to the typed X, Y" (when no field has been edited: "Click: nothing to apply, type X and Y first"). 44. (Proposal) Given the pointer on a mark, then the untouched fields show that point's current X and Y while the pointer is there (and the selected option's, when the pointer is on no mark), so the maker sees which point the numbers will mean; typed fields keep their text. A click then compares each field with the prefill of the point that was clicked ("unedited" is decided against that point).

Given an invalid field (not a number, or a result beyond +-1e7 mm on any edge), then the chip stays open, the field is marked invalid "Enter a number", focus goes to it, and nothing is written, as for Enter (`0010` criterion 22). The limit is checked on all four edges, so it is the same for every reference point.

Ctrl held at the click on the mark changes nothing: the Copy check at that moment is the state.
47. Given a rotated object, then the marks are at the corners and centre of the TBB, not of the oriented box, and the centre handle (at the oriented box's centre) and the centre cross can differ; the centre handle stays drawn in its dragging look while the chip is open (`0010` criterion 18) and a press on the centre cross wins over it.
48. Given a multi-selection or, later, a group, then the TBB is the group box of `0019` criterion 1, the five points are the group box's, and the move applies to every selected object by the same offset in one commit. A single object is not special.
49. Given the reference choice, then it is never stored: not in the document, not in the project file, not in a setting. It is reset to Centre when the chip opens. The chip still opens in Relative every time (`0010` criterion 18).
50. At a zoom where `s` is under 24 px the marks are not drawn and not clickable, and the Reference control is the way. Test: select a 2 mm square at 100 % zoom (`s` about 7.6 px): the red box is drawn, no mark is, and the Reference control with "Top left" selected and Enter moves the top-left corner.

## Out of scope

- A stored per-object matrix so that primitives stay primitives under skew (P3 of `0008`): still deferred; this spec converts. A skewed ellipse is mathematically still an ellipse; it is converted anyway (question 3).
- Non-uniform stretch of a **single** polygon or star, and any edge handle on it (question 2). A rounded rectangle stretched with sx and sy that differ keeps the single-radius rule of `0005` criterion 9 (question 15).
- A message, a setting, a "convert automatically" switch, or a way to turn the conversion off.
- Conversion by rotate, move, copy, uniform scale, Boolean operations (they take primitives as operands as before), save or export. Node-tool entry for a primitive by double-click (the hint "Nodes: Object to path, then double-click" of `0009` stays; question 12).
- Sliders that do not exist yet (palettes, brushes, a history scrubber). The value fields (`role="spinbutton"`) and the Points field (question 6). A new slider look, a wheel or scroll gesture on sliders, a numeric stepper.
- A value entry for hue or saturation of its own (question 5), an alpha slider.
- Edge-midpoint reference points (nine in all), a reference choice in the Relative mode, a remembered reference, a reference setting in a menu or the Properties panel, and a position readout in the panel (question 9).
- The red box during scale, rotate and skew drags, during a nudge (`0044`), during the typed rotate, size and skew chips, and in the Node tool (question 7). An "align to the page edge" command, distribute, and snapping the box to anything.
- A preview of the typed move on the canvas, and a clickable box (the box has no hit area).
- Undo (`0020`) beyond criterion 15.

## Open questions (defaults taken)

None blocks the build; each default is what the criteria say. Change any at the demo.

1. **Which objects convert (request 1).** *A (default):* on a skew, every primitive; on a stretch of a multi-selection, only the objects that cannot represent the result (an aligned rectangle stays a rectangle). *B:* every primitive in the selection converts on any skew or any stretch. B is easier to explain, but a rectangle stretched along the document axes would lose its width, height and corner-radius parameters for nothing. Recommendation A.
2. **A single polygon or star: free stretch with edge handles?** *A (default):* no; it keeps corner handles and a uniform drag (`0005` criterion 11). A star in a pair can then be stretched while a lone star cannot, which is a visible inconsistency. *B:* a single polygon or star gets the edge handles, and a non-uniform stretch converts it. Recommendation A for this slice, B as a small follow-up if the customer notices.
3. **A skewed ellipse or circle.** *A (default):* converted like every primitive (one rule). *B:* an ellipse sheared stays an ellipse with new radii and a rotation (exact, but a circle skewed by a pure shear then differs from every other skewed shape and needs a decomposition). Recommendation A.
4. **The first click of a double-click on a slider.** *A (default):* it counts as a click and its value stays (criterion 24); simple, and `0020` will make it a step that can be taken back. *B:* the first click only previews; it commits after the double-click interval (about 400 ms) unless a second click follows, which discards it and opens the entry. B protects the old value from an accidental first click, at the price of a delayed commit that every other event (a key, a save, a tool switch) must flush first. Recommendation A until `0020` exists.
5. **Value entry for the colour area and the hue slider.** *A (default):* a double-click puts the cursor into the hex field of the same colour block. *B:* a small hue field (0 to 360°) over the hue slider and S and V fields over the area. The hex field already takes any colour; B adds two entry surfaces for a number nobody types. Recommendation A.
6. **Are the value fields sliders?** *A (default):* no. They are spinbuttons (`0017` criteria 34 to 45): a click types, a drag scrubs, the bar behind the number is a display. *B:* treat them as sliders: a click on the bar sets the value at the pointer, a double-click types. B reverses the accepted "click to type" of `0017` and the tooltip "Drag to change, click to type". Recommendation A; say so if the customer meant them.
7. **Which drags show the red box (request 3).** *A (default):* the move drag only (move, axis lock, copy). *B:* every transform drag (scale, rotate, skew) and the typed chips of those. During a rotate the TBB changes shape every frame and is the answer to "how big will it get", which is useful but not asked. Recommendation A.
8. **Reference points.** *A (default):* five (four corners and the centre), as the customer wrote. *B:* nine (add four edge midpoints), as in Illustrator and Affinity. Edge midpoints suit "put the middle of the left edge on the 100 mm mark". Cheap to add later (two more marks and radios). Recommendation A.
9. **Does a remembered reference or a panel readout follow?** *A (default):* the reference resets to Centre at every open and is never stored; there is no panel X/Y, so nothing to follow. If a Position readout is added, it shows the Centre, offers the same five points and keeps its own choice for the session. *B:* a persistent reference shared by the chip and a future readout. Persistent hidden settings are the Illustrator complaint. Recommendation A.
10. **How pale is "pale red".** *A (default):* box line `--marquee-contain` (`#E5484D`) at 55 % with a 55 % white casing (about 2.0:1 on `--canvas-bg`, informational like the member box and the axis guides), marks at 100 % (3.2:1). *B:* the box line at 100 % (3.2:1, meets the 3:1 non-text rule everywhere, but not pale). *C:* a lighter 40 % (about 1.6:1). The box's information is also carried by the marks, the numbers and the dashed blue box, which is why A is acceptable; raise A toward 70 % if the screenshot review finds it under 1.5:1 on a fill. Recommendation A.
11. **A click on a mark with nothing typed.** *A (default):* closes the chip and writes nothing (criterion 45). *B:* keeps the chip open, so the click only selects the reference. B makes a second click necessary and conflicts with "clicking executes". Recommendation A.
12. **Double-click on a primitive.** The hint "Nodes: Object to path, then double-click" stays (`0009` criterion 32). *A (default):* unchanged. *B:* the double-click converts and opens the Node tool (`0009` question 3 (b), rejected then). Not asked; listed because the customer's rule "do not forbid or warn unnecessarily" could be read to include it. Recommendation A, ask if wanted.
13. **A mark covered by the chip.** The chip opens 16 px right of and below the centre handle; the lower-right mark lies at half the box size right of and below the centre, so any box up to about 470 x 260 px on screen has that mark under the chip. That is the usual case, not an edge case. *Decided by the UX review (2026-10-10):* the chip's top is 16 px below the lower edge of the box (UX notes, "Chip"), in both modes; a mark that is still covered (a box taller than the room below it, chrome over a mark) is not clickable, and the Reference control reaches it. No customer decision needed.
14. **Hit size of the marks.** 24 px across (radius 12), 44 px for touch, shrinking to `0.35 s` for small boxes (criterion 40). Default as written; the ux-engineer confirms against the 40-part laser layout of `0019` UX section 3.
15. **A rounded rectangle under a stretch.** *Default:* it stays a rectangle and its corner radius follows `0005` criterion 9 (the product `sqrt(sx * sy)`, an approximation `0019` already names). *Alternative:* convert it so the corners become exact ellipse quarters. Not asked.

## UX notes

Status: **drafted by the product owner on 2026-10-10 from the design system; reviewed and confirmed by the ux-engineer on 2026-10-10 (static review of the spec, the design system and the current chip and picker code; no screenshots yet)**. Corrections of that review are marked (UX). The measurements that need a rendered canvas stay as review gates for the build ("Known risks"). Sizes are screen pixels, colours are tokens of `docs/design-system.md` unless marked new.

### 1. Silent conversion

No new surface. What disappears: the "proportional only" corner-handle chip, the cause line, the skew gap for primitives, the K hint. What stays visible as feedback: the blue preview shows the sheared or stretched outline before the release (so the result of the conversion is seen, not described), and after the release the Select bar offers the node-related things a path has. No toast, no badge: the customer's rule is no warning, and the conversion is not an event the maker needs to be told about. A primitive's skew handles appear with the same offsets as a path's; the 14 px edge-handle depth trade-off of `0008` ("On a path the edge-midpoint resize handle is 14 px deep") now applies to every kind, so a rectangle's edge resize handle loses 8 px of reach (22 px to 14 px). (UX) Decided: 14 px for every kind. The point of the spec is that the kind no longer changes the handle set, and a per-kind depth would bring the kind back. The 40-part layout is a review gate of the build.
